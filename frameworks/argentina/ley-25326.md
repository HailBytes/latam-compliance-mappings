## Overview

Law 25.326 (*Ley de Protección de los Datos Personales*, LPDP) is Argentina's foundational personal data protection statute. Enacted in 2000 and substantially modeled on the EU Directive 95/46/EC (GDPR's predecessor), it established a comprehensive framework for the protection of personal data held in databases (*archivos*, *registros*, *bases de datos*, *bancos de datos*) — both public and private.

Argentina is one of the few non-EU countries to hold a **formal adequacy decision** from the European Commission, making it a preferred data routing hub for organizations managing EU-related data flows in the region.

---

## Scope

The law applies to:
- Personal data recorded in files, registers, or databases — whether public or private
- Any organization that collects, stores, processes, or transmits personal data of Argentine residents
- Extraterritorial application: organizations outside Argentina that process data of Argentine residents using means located in Argentina

Exemptions include: purely personal/household use, journalistic/artistic/literary expression, and state security/intelligence activities under specific conditions.

---

## Core Principles

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Legality** | Data may only be collected through lawful means; deceptive collection is prohibited |
| **Purpose limitation** | Data collected for one purpose may not be used for an incompatible purpose |
| **Data quality** | Data must be accurate, complete, up to date, and relevant to the stated purpose |
| **Security** | Appropriate technical and organizational measures must protect data from unauthorized access, loss, or alteration |
| **Confidentiality** | Persons who process data are bound by professional secrecy obligations |
| **Proportionality** | Only data necessary for the stated purpose may be collected |

## Data Subject Rights — "Habeas Data" (Art. 14–20)

Argentina's data protection framework is grounded in the constitutional right of *habeas data* (Art. 43 of the Argentine Constitution), which gives individuals the right to know what personal data is held about them and to demand its correction or deletion.

| Right | Description | Response Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Access** (*Acceso*) | Obtain confirmation of whether data is held and receive a copy | 30 calendar days |
| **Rectification** (*Rectificación*) | Correct inaccurate or outdated data | 5 business days after notification |
| **Update** (*Actualización*) | Update data that has become outdated | 5 business days |
| **Suppression** (*Supresión*) | Delete data that is unlawful, excessive, or no longer necessary | 5 business days |
| **Blocking** (*Bloqueo*) | Temporarily suspend processing during a dispute | — |
| **Confidentiality** (*Confidencialidad*) | Request data be treated as confidential | — |

If an organization fails to respond within the deadlines, the data subject may bring a *habeas data* action before a federal court.

## Sensitive Data (*Datos Sensibles*) — Art. 2, 7

Sensitive data is defined as data that reveals racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, union membership, health information, and sex life.

**Processing of sensitive data is generally prohibited** unless:
- The data subject provides explicit consent
- Processing is required for medical treatment, health research, or public health purposes
- Legal or regulatory obligations require it
- Processing is carried out by non-profit associations for their members

**Financial and credit data** (*datos de solvencia patrimonial*) is subject to additional specific rules under Art. 26: credit reporting databases may only retain adverse information for **5 years** from the originating event.

---

## Database Registration Requirement (Art. 21–24)

Unlike most modern frameworks, Law 25.326 requires **mandatory registration of databases** (*archivos*, *bases de datos*) with the AAIP:

- All public and private databases containing personal data must be registered
- Registration includes: name and address of the responsible party, purpose of the database, categories of data held, information on international transfers
- The AAIP maintains a public registry of databases (*Registro Nacional de Bases de Datos*)
- Unregistered databases are subject to sanctions

This registration requirement differentiates Argentina from LGPD (Brazil) and GDPR, which do not require blanket database registration.

---

## International Data Transfers (Art. 12)

International transfers (*transferencias internacionales*) are only permitted to countries or international organizations that provide an **adequate level of protection** comparable to Argentina's.

**Exceptions** (transfers may proceed without adequacy):
- Data subject has given express consent
- International judicial cooperation or public interest
- Vital interests of the data subject
- Transfer from public registers accessible to all
- Contract performance with the data subject
- Intragroup transfers with equivalent protection guarantees

Since Argentina holds EU adequacy, **data flows from EU to Argentina** can proceed without additional safeguards (SCCs, BCRs). This is a significant advantage for multinational organizations routing data through Argentina.

---

## AAIP — Enforcement Authority

The **Agencia de Acceso a la Información Pública (AAIP)** serves as Argentina's data protection authority. Established in 2016 (replacing the prior Dirección Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales), the AAIP has the following powers:

- Oversee compliance with Law 25.326
- Investigate complaints and initiate ex officio investigations
- Impose administrative sanctions
- Maintain the National Database Registry
- Issue guidelines and opinions
- Represent Argentina in international data protection forums

### Penalties

Violations are classified as minor, serious, or very serious:

| Category | Examples | Fine Range (ARS) |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | Formal/procedural violations, late ARCO responses | Up to ARS 100,000 |
| Serious | Unlawful processing, failure to register database | ARS 100,001 – 500,000 |
| Very Serious | Processing sensitive data without consent, unlawful transfers, obstruction of AAIP | ARS 500,001 – 3,000,000 |

Note: Sanctions in ARS are periodically updated by AAIP resolution to account for inflation.

## Pending Modernization

Argentina's Law 25.326 was enacted in 2000 based on the EU's 1995 Directive — predating GDPR by 18 years. Modernization has been under active discussion:

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 2018 | AAIP published draft reform proposal |
| 2021 | Executive branch submitted preliminary reform bill (*anteproyecto*) |
| 2022–2023 | Congressional debates on alignment with GDPR and LGPD, especially: consent modernization, data minimization, automated decision-making rights, breach notification timelines |
| Pending | Formal GDPR-aligned reform law not yet enacted as of 2024 |

Key proposed changes: explicit breach notification deadline (72h), right to explanation for automated decisions, stronger sanctions aligned with GDPR, and removal of the universal database registration requirement.

**Practical implication:** Organizations should design their Argentina compliance programs to GDPR standards now — the reform will likely require it, and doing so protects EU adequacy status.

## Key Takeaways for Enterprise Buyers

1. **Argentina has EU adequacy** — data flows from EU/EEA to Argentina can proceed without SCCs; this is a major operational advantage
2. **Database registration is required** — any database holding Argentine personal data must be registered with AAIP; audit your data map accordingly
3. **Habeas data is constitutionally protected** — data subject rights in Argentina have constitutional weight; take ARCO-equivalent requests seriously
4. **The law is being modernized** — design to GDPR standards now to future-proof your compliance program
5. **Credit data has specific rules** — adverse credit information cannot be retained beyond 5 years; relevant for any fintech or credit-related use case
6. **BYOC supports data sovereignty** — keeping data in Argentine cloud regions (AWS São Paulo, Azure Brazil South) supports the purpose-limitation and security principles
