## Overview

The Marco Civil da Internet (*Marco Civil*) is Brazil's Internet Civil Rights Framework — a foundational law establishing rights, duties, and the legal framework for the use of the internet in Brazil. Enacted before the LGPD, it provided the first statutory protections for internet users' privacy and established data retention obligations for internet service providers and application providers.

Sometimes called Brazil's "Internet Constitution," the Marco Civil was the result of an open, participatory drafting process and broke new ground globally for its explicit net neutrality provisions.

---

## Key Pillars

### 1. Net Neutrality (*Neutralidade de Rede*) — Art. 9

The Marco Civil established strong net neutrality protections:

- Internet access providers (*provedores de acesso*) must treat all data packets equally, without discrimination by origin, destination, content, service, or application
- Traffic management is permitted only to preserve network quality and security (e.g., responding to DDoS), not for commercial prioritization
- **Decree 8.771/2016** issued technical implementing rules, prohibiting paid prioritization and requiring ANATEL oversight

Brazil's net neutrality framework is considered one of the strongest in the world and has been cited in international policy debates.

### 2. Data Retention Obligations — Art. 13–15

The Marco Civil established mandatory minimum data retention periods:

| Provider Type | Data Type | Retention Period |
|---|---|---|
| **Connection providers** (*provedores de conexão*) — ISPs | Connection logs (IP address, date/time, duration) | **1 year** |
| **Application providers** (*provedores de aplicações*) — websites, apps, platforms | Access logs (IP address used to access the application) | **6 months** |

**Key points:**
- Retention is required regardless of user consent
- Data may only be provided to third parties pursuant to a **court order** (*ordem judicial*)
- Retention does not authorize use for any other purpose

These obligations were separate from and earlier than LGPD; they coexist with LGPD's broader data protection framework.

### 3. Privacy and Data Protection — Art. 7–11

Before the LGPD, the Marco Civil provided foundational user rights for internet use:

| Right | Article | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Inviolability of communications | Art. 7, I | Internet communications are private; interception requires court order |
| Data collection limitation | Art. 7, VII | Only data strictly necessary for the service may be collected |
| Purpose limitation | Art. 7, VIII | Data collected for one purpose may not be used for another |
| Informed consent | Art. 7, IX | Consent must be explicit and prior to collection; privacy policy must be accessible in Portuguese |
| Deletion on contract termination | Art. 7, X | Users may request deletion of personal data upon service termination |
| No transfer to third parties without consent | Art. 7, VII | Third-party data sharing requires separate consent |

### 4. Platform Liability — Art. 18–21

The Marco Civil established a safe harbor model for platform liability:

- **Connection providers** are not liable for content generated by users
- **Application providers** are generally not liable for user-generated content **unless** they fail to comply with a court order to remove specific content
- Exception: **Non-consensual intimate images** (revenge porn) — platforms must remove upon notification from the victim without requiring a court order (Art. 21)

## Interaction with LGPD

Since the LGPD came into force in September 2020, the relationship between the two laws is as follows:

| Issue | Governing Law | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Personal data processing generally | **LGPD** | LGPD is *lex specialis*; applies to all personal data processing |
| Internet connection log retention | **Marco Civil** (Art. 13) | Specific obligation; LGPD does not override it |
| Application log retention | **Marco Civil** (Art. 15) | Specific obligation remains in force |
| Consent for data collection online | **LGPD** | LGPD supersedes Marco Civil's consent provisions for personal data |
| Net neutrality | **Marco Civil** | Not covered by LGPD; Marco Civil and Decree 8.771/2016 govern |
| Platform liability for user content | **Marco Civil** | Not a data protection matter; Marco Civil governs |
| Court orders for data disclosure | **Marco Civil** | Procedure for log data; LGPD covers data subject access rights |

**Practical implication:** Organizations operating in Brazil must comply with **both** laws. The LGPD governs the processing of personal data with rights and obligations for controllers. The Marco Civil adds specific obligations around log retention and net neutrality that exist independently of the LGPD's framework.

---

## Key Takeaways for Enterprise Buyers

1. **Log retention is mandatory and time-bound** — connection logs 1 year, application logs 6 months; these are floor minimums under a court order mechanism
2. **Disclosure requires a court order** — user data from logs cannot be shared with third parties (including law enforcement) without judicial authorization
3. **Net neutrality is law** — network service providers (and B2B customers relying on them) must ensure traffic is treated neutrally
4. **LGPD takes primacy on personal data** — for privacy compliance programs, LGPD is the primary framework; Marco Civil adds specific internet-context obligations on top
5. **Platform liability safe harbor is conditional** — companies operating user-generated content platforms must have a working court order compliance process
6. **Privacy policy in Portuguese is required** — consent under Marco Civil Art. 7 requires the privacy notice to be written in accessible Portuguese
